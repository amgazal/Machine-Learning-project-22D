The Problem

Bots are everywhere.
Telling them apart is hard.

An estimated 10–20% of Twitter/X accounts are automated bots, spiking to 43% during the 2020 US election. These bots spread misinformation, distort public opinion research, and influence financial markets — yet no major platform has integrated reliable AI text detection.

🗳️

Election Integrity

15% of pro-Trump and 7% of pro-Biden posts during the 2020 election were driven by coordinated bot accounts.

🦠

Public Health

During COVID-19, bots flooded Twitter with anti-vaccine conspiracies linked to real-world vaccine hesitancy.

📉

Financial Markets

AI-generated fake content has been shown to cause instantaneous stock market losses of hundreds of billions of dollars.

🔬

Research Integrity

Researchers studying public opinion on Twitter cannot trust their findings if a significant portion of data was bot-generated.

Project Evolution

How we got here.

  • Started with Disaster Tweet Classification

    Built a model to filter relevant tweets during natural disasters using 165,000+ labeled tweets from Kaggle and Zenodo.

  • Identified critical gaps

    The dataset did not clearly prove the problem existed in practice. No direct evidence that emergency responders were actually overwhelmed by tweet volume. Unclear target audience and no realistic deployment path.

  • Explored alternatives

    Investigated source credibility detection, Ushahidi crisis classification and fake review detection before finding the right direction.

  • Pivoted to AI Tweet Detection

    Settled on detecting AI-generated tweets — a well-documented problem with clear use cases for researchers, election monitors and journalists. Built and compared three models with BERT achieving 92% accuracy.

Methodology

Three models. One clear winner.

We followed a baseline-to-advanced progression — starting simple to understand the data, then scaling up to a state-of-the-art transformer model.

01
Logistic Regression
Supervised · TF-IDF Features

Our baseline. Uses TF-IDF to convert tweet words into numerical features — giving high scores to rare meaningful words and low scores to common ones. Fast, interpretable and a strong benchmark. We compared unigram vs bigram approaches finding bigrams improved performance.

82.35%
Accuracy
02
Random Forest
Supervised · Ensemble · TF-IDF Features

An ensemble of 100 decision trees trained on TF-IDF features. Captures more complex patterns than Logistic Regression. Achieved the highest recall (91.87%) — catching more AI tweets — but lower precision, meaning it also flagged more human tweets incorrectly.

82.82%
Accuracy
03
BERT
Supervised · Deep Learning · Transformer

Fine-tuned on our combined dataset for 3 epochs using a T4 GPU on Google Colab. Unlike simpler models, BERT reads the full tweet in both directions — understanding context rather than just counting words. It can distinguish "this party was fire 🔥" from "building is on fire" where simpler models fail.

★ Best Model

Essential Question: How can AI/ML amplify or mitigate bias?

Our model partially learned election vocabulary from ElectAI rather than general AI writing patterns — a form of topic bias that could cause inconsistent performance on tweets outside election domains. We mitigated this by combining two diverse datasets, applying stratified splits, using class weight balancing, and transparently reporting per-class performance rather than just headline accuracy numbers.

Impact

Positive & Negative Impact

✓ Positive Impact
  • Helps researchers studying public opinion filter bot content for more accurate findings
  • Supports content moderation by helping platforms monitor AI-generated posts
  • Promotes AI transparency by helping users identify machine-generated content
✗ Negative Impact
  • False positives may incorrectly flag human tweets as AI-generated, silencing real voices
  • Model may perform differently across writing styles, languages and demographics
  • Could be misused to unfairly judge people based on how they write
